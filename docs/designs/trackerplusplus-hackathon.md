## Problem Statement

People don't skip workouts for lack of a good plan. They skip because life gets in the way, and the first missed workout is the moment most likely to turn into quitting. TrackerPlusPlus is an iOS personal agent that protects workout slots before they're lost, recovers the week after a miss, and uses reward designs that published studies found effective, so the person does 10% more workouts than their own 4-week normal.

Built for the Personal Agents Hackathon (Friday, November 20, San Francisco): hacking 11:00 AM to the 4:30 PM submission, finalist demos at 5:00 PM. Team of four: iOS (doc owner), Tony (data and memory), Surya (planner), Anna (design). Team plan: https://claude.ai/artifact/1EL4tjDc5G2eSbDqYAUK5o

## What Makes This Cool

- The hero moment is a failure. The demo clock jumps to 1:30 PM on the demo day, the agent sees Sam missed his lunchtime walk, shrinks the next one to 15 minutes, doubles its points and puts it on his calendar this evening, all without being asked (D6).
- It acts before a miss too. A meeting lands on tomorrow morning's workout and the agent moves it to the next free slot and says why in its feed (D6).
- Pitch line: "Google and Apple coach your workout. We make sure it happens."
- Every rule traces to a study: comeback bonuses and higher rewards (Nature 2021 megastudy), loss-framed points (Annals of Internal Medicine 2016), immediate rewards and adaptive goals (BMC Public Health 2017), acting on the first miss (PNAS 2023).

## Constraints

- 5½ hours of hacking on Friday. Prep from now through Thursday is allowed.
- iOS only. One iOS developer is the bottleneck, so harness engineers own every rule that can live server-side.
- Memory lives on the phone. The harness stores nothing between calls.
- Any workout of 10+ minutes counts. Completed workouts are imported automatically, with no manual check-in.
- Demo target: 10% more workouts than the 4-week baseline, rounded up over 4 weeks. People already at 6+ per week are held at their current count with a 90% completion goal, enforced in code.
- Weekly training load (effort × minutes) is capped, so adding workouts never means piling on intensity. See the next section.

## Training Load Guardrail

The planner keeps each week's planned load within two limits, both enforced in code:

1. Planned 7-day load is at most 110% of the larger of last week's load and the 28-day weekly average, so one light week doesn't shrink the next (D15).
2. The 7-day load stays at or below 1.3× the 28-day weekly average. A ratio of 1.5 or above is flagged as high risk.

Load for one workout = effort (1–10) × minutes. Effort comes from HealthKit's `workoutEffortScore`, or `estimatedWorkoutEffortScore` if the user didn't rate the workout (iOS 18 / watchOS 11 and later). Workouts without either score get an estimate from average heart rate. This mirrors Apple Watch's own Training Load, which compares the last 7 days with the previous 28.

When more workouts and the load cap conflict, the planner keeps the extra workout and makes it shorter or easier. For Sam, the 10% goal adds about one workout a month, so his planned load barely changes.

What the evidence supports, stated honestly in the pitch as a cautious guardrail, not proven injury prevention:

- Gabbett (Br J Sports Med, 2016) recommends limiting weekly load increases to under 10%. That rests on unpublished data from professional rugby league players, where weeks up 15% or more carried a 21–49% injury risk. The same paper names a 7-day to 28-day ratio of 0.8–1.3 as the "sweet spot" and 1.5 or above as the "danger zone." https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4789704/
- Buist et al. (Am J Sports Med, 2008), the only randomized trial of the 10% rule, found no difference in injuries among 532 novice runners: 20.8% on a graded 10%-rule plan vs 20.3% on a standard plan. https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/20220
- Nielsen et al. (JOSPT, 2014), 874 novice runners: injury rates didn't differ overall. Increases over 30% in two weeks showed a non-significant hint of more distance injuries (hazard ratio 1.59, p = .07). https://vbn.aau.dk/da/publications/excessive-progression-in-weekly-running-distance-and-risk-of-runn

**Choosing the workout from load.** Recommendations come from workouts the person has already done, never new ones. The planner reads the 7-day to 28-day load ratio:

| Load ratio | State | What the agent recommends |
|---|---|---|
| Below 0.8 | Low | A high-effort workout from their history, such as strength training or their long run |
| 0.8 to 1.3 | Steady | Their usual mix, weighted toward what they complete most often |
| Above 1.3 | High | A recovery day: yoga, or a light outdoor walk if they have no yoga history |

"From their history" means an activity type they've completed at least twice in the last 4 weeks, ranked by average effort. Walking is always allowed as a recovery option. These bands follow Gabbett's 0.8–1.3 range; the mapping from band to workout type is our own rule, not a study finding.

## Routine Pre-Scheduling

When someone already has a routine, the agent puts it on the calendar for them without asking. A routine is the same activity type on the same weekday, starting within the same 1-hour window, in at least 3 of the last 4 weeks. For example: swimming every Wednesday from 5 to 6 PM.

- Routines go on the TrackerPlusPlus calendar as recurring events and count toward the weekly goal. The planner fills the remaining gap around them.
- If a meeting lands on a routine, slot defense moves it, the same as any planned workout.
- A routine that's missed 2 weeks in a row is taken off the calendar and the feed says so, rather than the agent nagging.
- Why it should work: the habit study in the doc (PNAS 2023) found 69% of gym-goers were more likely to go on the same weekdays. Locking in what already works protects the habit.
- Demo: Jordan's training plan is the clearest example. The mock data needs his runs to start at consistent times each weekday, which the generator doesn't do yet.

## Premises

1. The hero moment is the miss: the agent notices a skipped workout by itself, shrinks the next one, doubles its points and reschedules it. Plan generation is supporting cast.
2. At a personal-agents hackathon the agent must visibly act for the user: it owns a calendar, moves workouts on it, and keeps a feed of what it did.
3. Pitch: "Google and Apple coach your workout. We make sure it happens." Google Health Coach, Apple Workout Buddy, Fitbod and WHOOP compete on plan quality or in-workout coaching; none acts on your calendar.
4. Cut for time: Fitbit, coach chat and leaderboards. Ten screens become five: your normal, this week, today, done, missed one.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Miss loop only
Demo clock, rule-based miss recovery, cached plans, an action feed. Ruled out as the whole plan because it reads as an app rather than an agent; kept as priority P0, the first milestone.

### Approach B: Full plan as written
Real HealthKit data, background import, on-phone memory, stateless Opus planner, points, reminders. Kept as priorities P2 and P3.

### Approach C: Slot defender
The agent owns a TrackerPlusPlus calendar, defends slots before a miss and recovers after one. Kept as priority P1.
