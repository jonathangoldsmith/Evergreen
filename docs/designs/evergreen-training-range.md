## Problem Statement

Evergreen keeps each person's training in their own range. It acts on the plan in both directions, the same way each time: change the session, ask first, offer Undo.

- **The brake (committed runners).** People who train seriously get hurt by doing too much too soon, or by training through fatigue they already notice. They don't lack information: they know they're tired and go anyway. Evergreen changes the planned session at that moment, before the run, rather than show another score.
- **The push (newcomers).** People newer to fitness drift below their own normal: one missed workout becomes a lost week. Evergreen acts on the first miss with a shorter comeback in their next free slot, sets a goal just above their normal, and rewards a workout the moment it lands.

Calendar misses (a meeting landing on a run) were the hackathon's hero feature. They stay as a supporting capability in both directions, not the pitch.

## Demand Evidence

- **First-hand only so far.** "We are the users." The founder's last missed workout: "Too tired or sore." What they did: "Pushed through anyway." What it cost: "An injury or time off."
- **No outside users yet.** The app has run on demo personas in a simulator; real Apple Health reading landed today (PR 24) and hasn't been on a real person's phone.
- **Claimed reach, unverified.** The founder says they can name 3+ people like them, but did not name them in the session.
- **The push side has study evidence but no real person.** The hackathon design grounded it in studies: comeback bonuses and higher rewards (Nature 2021 megastudy), loss-framed points (Annals of Internal Medicine 2016), immediate rewards and adaptive goals (BMC Public Health 2017), acting on the first miss (PNAS 2023). Its persona, Sam, was mock data. No newcomer has asked for it yet.

This is founder-as-user evidence, which is a real starting point but not demand. The assignment exists to change that.

## Status Quo

The founder's own workaround is nothing: notice fatigue, run anyway. In the market, readiness scores (WHOOP, Oura, Garmin) tell you how recovered you are, and adaptive plans (Runna, TrainAsOne and others) adjust the plan. Research found:

- No controlled evidence that readiness scores change athlete behavior.
- February 2026 coverage that Runna's adaptive plans pushed some runners into injury, and the app easing up since.
- A 2025 BJSM cohort of about 5,200 runners: a single run much longer than the longest run in the previous 30 days predicted injury (a 10–30% jump raised the injury rate 64%). Weekly acute:chronic ratios and weekly-change rules showed little or no association.
- A 2025 Journal of Athletic Training study (20,425 sessions): an injury in the previous 12 months was a more consistent warning sign than workload spikes.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Brake side:**

- **First users:** recreational runners training for a race on a fixed date (half or full marathon), who have been hurt before and follow a plan (Runna, a coach, or their own). A paid race and a deadline make an injury expensive.
- **First cohort (about 4):** the founder and the 3+ runners they can name, through TestFlight. **Second cohort (10):** each of those runners brings two from their club or Strava group.
- **Wedge:** one rule they can feel. The night before or morning of a run that's much longer than their longest in 30 days, or after they tap "Sore", Evergreen offers a specific shorter run and they accept or keep it.

**Push side:**
- **First users:** 3 newcomers the founder knows: people who work out under twice a week and want more. They're recruited alongside the runners.
- **Wedge:** the first miss. The evening of a missed workout, a 15-minute comeback is already on their calendar for the next free slot, worth 2× points, with Undo.


## Constraints

- Four-person team, coming out of a one-day hackathon; no outside users or revenue yet.
- iOS and watchOS (SwiftUI, HealthKit, EventKit, WatchConnectivity; the watch app is item 19). The harness is a FastAPI service on Surya's Vercel account.
- Apple Health strength workouts carry no weight, sets or reps, so a spike rule for them would run on noise.
- Plans synced from Runna or a coach usually arrive as a read-only calendar subscription: Evergreen can read them but can't change them.
- **Health data stays private by default.** It leaves the phone only under the anonymous per-device ID, with a delete route (shipped in PR 24). It reaches another person only when the user explicitly shares it:
  - the coach link (item 20): swaps, reasons and the weekly report, with pain answers only if the runner opts in;
  - buddy notes (item 22): completed workouts only.
  - Injury location and the "where" of a pain answer never leave the phone.
- **Health data leaves the phone only with consent (business notes decision BN2).** Onboarding's "What leaves your phone" step asks before anything is uploaded, as Washington's health data law requires separate consent to collect. Research use is a second, separate opt-in that's off by default. Both can be changed in Your data.
- **A push token is a new identifier.** Buddy notes use Apple push (item 22). The token is stored under the anonymous ID and removed on unpair, on DELETE, and when APNs reports it invalid.
- **The harness needs a persistent store.** SingleStore is required in production for guardrail events, coach shares, buddy pairs and push tokens. Without it the harness keeps data in memory and loses it on a cold start, so it refuses to create a share, a buddy invite or a push token (503) when no persistent store is configured. The deployed harness must have SingleStore before the first cohort, because guardrail events still fall back to memory. `/coach/{token}` is registered before the catch-all route in `harness/api/index.py`.

## Premises

1. **The product keeps training in each person's range, in both directions.** For people over their range (injury risk), it holds them back; for people under it (newcomers drifting), it brings them back. The brake is where the founder's own evidence is; the push rests on published studies and needs its own first users. Calendar misses are supporting.
2. **The product acts on the plan, not a score.** It shortens, swaps, moves or adds a session, asks first or offers Undo, and never guilts: no minus points, no red at a miss (DESIGN.md). One exception, approved in the CEO review (X5): after an injury, and only with the person's yes, it writes a short return-to-running plan (item 21). This overrides the second opinion's "skip plan generation" for that program only.
3. **The rule follows the evidence.** The signal is the one the 2025 cohort supports: a run longer than the longest run in the previous 30 days, weighted by recent injury. It replaces the weekly 110% / 1.3× cap as the main signal. The exact thresholds (below) are provisional heuristics, calibrated by the backtest, not numbers taken from the studies.
4. **Runs only in v1.** The rule's interface stays general internally. Rides come next, then other types, each only once its flags prove accurate with real users. This was originally "all training types"; it was revised after the second opinion showed Apple Health's strength data can't support the rule.
5. **Distribution starts with TestFlight** to the founder and the runners they know, not the App Store.

## Cross-Model Perspective

An independent Claude subagent cold-read a summary of the session (Codex isn't installed here).

- **Steelman:** "an injury guardrail that sits between whatever plan an athlete follows (Runna, a human coach, their own) and the session they are about to do… a second opinion on the coach that has the power to change the plan."
- **Key insight:** "Pushed through anyway." The founder lacked an easy alternative at the moment of decision, not information. So a one-tap soreness check and a specific shorter run are required, and the north-star metric is the swap acceptance rate.
- **Code contradicts the evidence:** the harness and app use a weekly 110% / 1.3× cap, which is ACWR-style logic the 2025 cohort didn't support.
- **Challenged premise #4:** "all training types". The founder accepted the revision to runs first.
- **48-hour prototype:** a backtest screen ("you'd have been flagged on these 6 days"), a three-question onboarding, plans read from the calendar, a morning check, and logging of every flag and choice. Skip shoes, the day pass, meeting moves and plan generation.

## Approaches Considered

- **Approach A, guardrail in the existing app.** The rule on the phone, the morning check, the swap and the backtest, using Evergreen's own plan or manual entry. Smallest; not chosen because it doesn't sit on top of the plans runners already follow.
- **Approach B, guardrail on top of any plan.** Chosen; see below.
- **Approach C, concierge backtest.** A script plus hand-sent reports and texts, no app changes. Not chosen as the build, though getting the runners' data is the assignment either way.

### What it does

Built in four milestones:

- **Milestone 1, the brake (phone first).** Items 1–9, plus the weekly pain check-in (17), the Sunday range report (18) and the not-medical-advice copy (item 9). The swap and the report work without a server. Events are uploaded to the harness when it's reachable. Actionable notifications (item 7) are new work. **First steps (eng review D4, D5):**
  - real people's decisions stop being cleared at midnight (the story-day reset stays for demo personas only);
  - the SwiftData store gets versioned schemas and a migration plan, and never deletes a real person's data on failure.

  About 5–6 human weeks, including the eng review's D2–D5 work.
- **Milestone 2, the push.** Items 10–16 plus the accountability buddy and its Apple push (22). Starts once the newcomer calls in The Assignment confirm the first-miss story; if they don't, the push and the buddy both wait. About 5 human weeks, most of it the buddy, its push and background workout detection.
- **Milestone 3, the harness and coaches.** Ports the brake rule into the harness's 7-day planner, and adds the coach view (20) with its public, token-scoped route, the T3 fixture pipeline, the tests and CI from the polish stage, and per-device auth replacing the shared harness secret (eng review D7). About 4–5 human weeks.
- **Milestone 4, the return and the wrist.** The return-to-running plan (21) and the Apple Watch app (19). Starts after milestone 1, because both reuse its rule and actions. About 2–3 human weeks.

Milestones 3 and 4 can run alongside milestone 2. The Swift/Python fixture pipeline that keeps the phone's rule and the harness's rule in step doesn't exist yet (contract test T3 is unbuilt). It's new work in milestone 3, and it also checks that the phone and the harness compute the same range report.

**1. Which events are planned runs.**
- **Training calendars:** in onboarding the person picks their training calendar(s). Calendars named like Runna, TrainingPeaks, Final Surge or "Training" are pre-selected. Events on calendars they didn't pick are never parsed.
- **Run test:** on a picked calendar, an event is a planned run when its title has a run word (run, long, easy, tempo, interval(s), threshold, recovery, fartlek, hills, track, race) or a distance (km, mi, k). Other events on those calendars are ignored.
- **Size:** the title gives a distance ("Long run 18 km") or a duration ("Easy 45 min"). If it has neither, a timed event's own length counts as the duration.
- **All-day events:** an all-day event never takes its length as a duration. Without a size in the title, it's shown but never flagged. With one, it's treated as an 8 AM run for timing, and its swap is a timed event at 8 AM with the suggested size (plan items have no all-day form).
- **Not busy time:** events on picked training calendars are left out of slot defense's busy times, so a Runna run is never treated as a meeting. This gets a slot-defense test.
- **One plan per day:** on a day with an external planned run, Evergreen's own HistoryPlan item for that day is hidden. The external plan wins; days are matched by calendar day. The exception is an active return plan (item 21): its session wins, and the external run shows as "Paused for your return plan (Runna says 18 km)". The external event itself is never touched.

**2. What the spike rule can and can't see.**
- **Plans that come from history barely spike.** HistoryPlan sizes each workout to the person's median, so it almost never exceeds their 30-day longest. For people without an external plan, only the Sore and Tired flags fire.
- **The first cohort needs an external plan.** It's drawn from runners who already follow one (Runna, a coach), and the success criteria are measured on them.

**3. Comparing like with like.**
- **Distance first:** distance to distance when the plan has a distance and the history runs have one.
- **Otherwise duration:** duration to duration. A Health run without a distance counts only in duration comparisons.
- **Matching units:** the swap card uses the plan's unit, km for a distance plan and min for a duration plan.

**4. Onboarding, in this order.**
- **Order (design review decision 3):**
  1. Welcome
  2. Permissions (Health, calendar, notifications)
  3. What leaves your phone (consent, item 9, business notes decision BN2)
  4. pick training calendars (item 1)
  5. injury question
  6. backtest
  7. the return-plan offer, if the injury was recent
- **Not medical advice, beside the injury question:** one muted line under it: "Evergreen suggests training changes. It isn't medical advice; see a physio or doctor for pain or injury." Not a separate first screen.
- **Injury question:** "Any injury in the last 12 months? No / Yes" and, if yes, roughly when (month). The location stays on the phone. If the injury was in the last 3 months, onboarding ends by offering the return plan (item 21, once milestone 4 ships).
- **Then the backtest.** A one-time HealthKit query reads the last 395 days of runs (365 to check, plus 30 for the first lookback). It's separate from HealthReader's 60-day read and isn't cached beyond its result. The raw runs are never uploaded. Each quiz answer is uploaded as a `backtest_answer` event (eng re-run D1; see item 9). While it runs: "Checking your last year…".
- **Which threshold applies:** each past day uses the tighter threshold if it falls within 12 months after the reported injury month, and the base one otherwise.
- **Asked blind, then revealed (design review decision 5):**
  - **The questions:** up to 8, with "Skip the rest". If more than 4 days were flagged, a random 4 of them are mixed with 4 random unflagged days. Each shows a plain date and run ("Aug 17, a 24 km run"), with no jump percentage: "Were you hurt or sore for days within 2 weeks? Yes / No / Don't know".
  - **The reveal:** only after the questions, "I'd have stepped in on N days", with the flagged list and each jump.
  - **None flagged:** "Your last year stayed in range."
- **Too little history, or Health not allowed (design review decision 6):** with fewer than 20 runs in the 395 days, one message covers both cases, since HealthKit returns a refusal as empty: "Not enough history to check yet. I'll watch from today. If you didn't allow Apple Health, you can turn it on in Settings." It has an "Open Settings" ghost button.

**5. The rule (thresholds provisional).**
- **Spike:** a planned run more than 10% past the longest run in the previous 30 days, matching the cohort's lowest risk band. After an injury in the last 12 months, more than 5%. "Injury" means the onboarding answer or a "Yes" to the weekly pain question (item 17, X9), whichever is more recent.
- **No recent runs to compare:** with fewer than 2 comparable runs in the previous 30 days (back from a break, or none measured in the plan's unit), there are no spike flags. The one exception: after an injury in the last 12 months, any planned run over 30 minutes or 5 km is flagged as a return-to-running spike. Sore and Tired still apply. Return-plan sessions (item 21) are exempt from this flag and from the spike rule, because they're generated under both limits.
- **Sore:** the person taps "Sore" and the planned run is long: 60+ minutes, 10+ km, or the longest planned run in the next 7 days.
- **Tired:** the person taps "Tired" and the run is long (same definition) or hard. Hard means the title has tempo, interval(s), threshold, repeats, hills, fartlek, track, VO2 or race pace.
- **One flag per run.** A flag fires once per run and condition, so Undo and Keep stick.

**6. What the swap suggests.**
- **Spike:** at most the 30-day longest run, or that minus 10% after an injury, unless a floor below lifts it. The floor wins over the cap. A return-to-running spike suggests 30 minutes or 5 km.
- **Sore:** the planned run cut by 30% and made easy.
- **Tired:** cut by 20% and made easy, with any intensity dropped.
- **Floors:** never below 20 minutes for a duration plan or 3 km for a distance plan. For Sore and Tired only: if the cut would be under 10%, the suggestion is "Keep the distance, run it easy." A spike always suggests the shorter run. If a floor would lift the spike suggestion to the planned run's size or above, the spike isn't flagged at all.

**7. When it asks.**
- **Morning check:** on run days, a local notification with Fresh / Tired / Sore actions at 7 AM or 60 minutes before the run, whichever is earlier, never before 5 AM. For runs starting before 6 AM, it's asked at 8 PM the evening before. The same card shows on Today until the run starts.
- **Spike flags:** notified at 7 PM the evening before a run that starts before noon, and at 7 AM for later runs. Rounded numbers in the text; no title from another calendar.
- **Notification budget (design review decision 10):**
  - at most 2 guardrail notifications a day;
  - an evening spike note says "Tomorrow's 18 km is a big jump from your longest run (13 km). Make it 12?";
  - no morning check is sent once that run's swap is decided;
  - Sunday's pain question and range report go out as one 6 PM notification: "How was your week? Any pain?" with No / A little / Yes. "No" is recorded in the background; "A little" and "Yes" record the answer and open the app to the OSTRC questions (item 17).
  - **Counting and the Sunday rule (eng re-run D3):**
    - the cap counts by the day a note fires;
    - the Sunday note is never dropped, and a competing evening spike note is folded into its text;
    - plan reminders (30 minutes before a session) aren't guardrail notes. A run that got a morning check skips its reminder.
- **From the Lock Screen:** "Make it 12 km" applies in the background by recording the same swap action as tapping in the app; CalendarSync then writes the event, so there is no separate direct EventKit write. It posts a confirmation with an Undo action. If the background work fails, the action opens the app instead. The notification's userInfo carries the full swap (run key, planned and suggested size, unit), because Apple Health can't be read while the phone is locked. The action records it without re-running the rule.
- **New work:** the app defines no notification categories or actions yet (`Notifications/` has none), so the action buttons are built in milestone 1. These same notifications appear on a paired watch before the watch app (item 19) ships.

**8. What accepting does.**
- **Evergreen's own run:** a swap action changes the plan item, as moves and shrinks do today.
- **Decisions last (eng review D4):** for real people, swaps, Keeps, Undos, pain answers, the injury month and return-plan state are never cleared at midnight. Today `clearRun` (AppModel.swift:515) deletes every action on each new day; that reset now applies to demo personas only.
- **New model fields (contract change):** `PlanItem.Source` gains `.swap`, and a swap plan item carries `swapOf` (the source run's matching key, its planned size and unit). `ActionRecord` must store these new fields too, since it rebuilds actions field by field and silently drops anything it doesn't store.
- **An external run (Runna, a coach):** the source event is never touched. Accepting records an action that adds a plan item `swap-<hash of the source run's matching key>` at the same start time. The matching key is the source calendar's identifier, the run's start day and its title, never `eventIdentifier` (which the calendar code deliberately doesn't trust). The swap is titled "Easy run 12 km (was 18 km)". CalendarSync writes it like any plan item under the existing key (`evergreen://plan/<day>/swap-…`), so later syncs keep it. The card says "Runna will still say 18 km."
- **Undo** marks that action undone, and the next sync removes the event, as Undo works today. If no run on the source calendar matches the key any more (moved, renamed or deleted), Evergreen records its own cancel action (like `cancelPurchase` today), not the person's Undo, so the change isn't read as a refusal and the replacement run can be flagged again.

**9. Logging and privacy.**
- **On the harness:** each flag, morning answer, choice (accept / keep / undo) and the run actually done go to a new `guardrail_events` table under the anonymous per-device ID. So does each weekly pain answer (No / A little / Yes / no answer), with its four OSTRC answer codes when they were given (item 17). Injury is sent as yes/no and months ago, never the location. Pain answers never include where. A swap event carries the planned size, the suggested size and the 30-day longest (rounded, in the plan's unit) and its reason kind, which is what the coach page shows.
- **One upload path (eng review D1).** The phone keeps one durable outbox (`EventOutbox`) for every uploaded event: flags, choices, pain answers, misses, comebacks, `workout_completed`, `range_week`, `backtest_answer` and `consent` (BN2). A `backtest_answer` carries yes / no / don't know, whether the day was flagged or a control, the jump percent rounded to 5, and the months ago. It carries no date or distance (eng re-run D1). It sends them to one route, `POST /users/{user}/events`, as typed events with a client-generated id. The harness validates each event type and ignores ids it has already seen, so a retry never doubles an event. It fails closed: a 503 without SingleStore, and an error status on any write failure. It returns the ids it stored, and the outbox acknowledges only those, so an event is never lost to a quiet failure.
- **New harness tables (five):**
  - `guardrail_events`: every event type above (`workout_completed` for paired people only, with type and rounded minutes);
  - `coach_shares`: item 20;
  - `buddy_pairs`: item 22, holding invites too, with status invited, paired or ended;
  - `push_tokens`: item 22;
  - `devices`: per-device auth (eng review D7), holding each install's token hash.
- **Consent (business notes decision BN2):**
  - **The screen:** onboarding's "What leaves your phone" step lists, in plain words, what's uploaded (flags, choices, weekly pain answers with their OSTRC answers, injury as yes/no and months ago, backtest answers, week counts) and what never is (raw Health data, injury or pain location, calendar titles). Primary button: "Allow and continue". Ghost button: "Keep it on my phone".
  - **Declining:** nothing is uploaded and the outbox holds nothing. The brake, the swap and the range report still work, because they run on the phone. Sharing a coach link or inviting a buddy needs uploads, so those screens ask for this consent first.
  - **Research opt-in:** one tick on the same screen, off by default: "Let researchers use my answers, without my name, to study running injuries." It's separate from the upload consent and can be turned off at any time.
  - **The record:** each consent choice is a `consent {uploads: yes | no, research: yes | no, version}` event with its time, kept on the phone and sent through the outbox only once uploads are allowed (a "no" to uploads never leaves the phone). Uploads begin only after a "yes" is recorded. Research exports include only people whose latest consent event says research yes; turning it off removes them from later exports. DELETE erases these events with the rest.
  - **Existing users:** the eng re-run's one-time catch-up flow (R8) starts with this step, since PR 24 already uploads their history.
  - **The buddy's friend:** the short path (item 22) includes this step, because pairing uploads a push token.
  - **Before any research use:** a lawyer's read on Apple guideline 5.1.3 and the state health data laws, and the research partner's ethics approval, as the business notes say.
- **Wellness wording (business notes decision BN3):** every app screen, notification, the coach page and App Store and marketing copy say what Evergreen does ("in range", "a big jump from your longest run", "shortened"), never that it makes running safe. No "safe", "prevents injury", "lowers your risk" or "injury-proof". The not-medical-advice line stays as it is.
- **Delete covers every one.** `GET` and `DELETE /users/{user}` and the Your data screen cover all five tables. `store.delete_user` today erases only `user_history`, `recommendations` and `verifications`, so it's extended, with a test per table.
  - **When one side of a pair deletes:** the pair and that person's push token are removed. The other person's own data is untouched; their buddy row disappears and their notes stop.
  - **When a runner deletes:** each coach link shows "This link is no longer active."
- **Amplitude rules:**
  - `guardrail_flag_shown {kind: spike | check_in}`
  - `guardrail_choice {choice: accept | keep | undo}`
  - `backtest_completed {answered: n}`, never the answers (eng re-run D1)
  - `pain_checkin {answered: true | false}`, never the answer
  - `range_report_viewed`
  - `watch_action {kind: check_in | swap}`, never the answer
  - `coach_share {action: created | revoked}`
  - no return-plan event at all: starting one would reveal an injury or a pain answer
  - `buddy {action: paired | unpaired}`

  No distances, no soreness, no pain answers, no injury details, no calendar titles.
- **Not medical advice** appears in four places, owned by the founder and in place before the second cohort or any newcomer build: onboarding (item 4), the footer of the Why screen, the return plan's entry card (item 21) and the coach page footer (item 20). This closes R3-18.

### The push side (newcomers)

Mostly restored, not new: the adaptive goal and points totals are still in `StandInAgent`, and the comeback booking and Done sheet were removed in commit `9179c3b` and can be brought back from history.

**10. Who gets pushed.**
- **The push** applies when someone's last 4 weeks average under 2 workouts a week, or they have under 14 days of history (the existing "getting to know you" starter).
- **The brake** applies to anyone whose next run spikes.
- **Never both on one workout:** a comeback is always shorter than the person's median, so it can't trigger a spike.

**11. The goal.** Their normal plus 10%, rounded up (existing rule), shown as "This week: 1 of 3 done."

**12. Acting on the first miss.**
- **When it's a miss:** a planned workout with no matching Apple Health workout by 30 minutes after it ends.
- **The comeback:** Evergreen books one comeback at about 60% of their median length (at least 10 minutes) in the next free calendar slot within 24 hours, on Evergreen's calendar, for 2× points, with Undo and "Pick another time".
- **Limits:** one comeback per miss; a missed comeback isn't chased.

**13. Points, framed to keep.**
- **Earning:** each workout earns points; a comeback earns double.
- **The copy:** says what a workout keeps: "Keep your 40 points: a 15-minute walk tonight."
- **No losses shown:** points never go below zero and a miss shows no minus number.

**14. The payoff.** When an Apple Health workout lands, a Done sheet shows the points, and the pine buddy fills a tier and celebrates.

**15. Usual days.** The week stays on the weekdays and times they already go (HistoryPlan).

**16. Push logging.**
- **Harness:** misses, comebacks offered, accepted, undone and completed go to the same `guardrail_events` table, covered by the same delete route.
- **Amplitude** gets `comeback_booked` and `comeback_done` with no durations or health values, plus the buddy event in item 9.

### Added in the CEO review (items 17–22)

Each traces to a ledger row (X1–X8) below.

**17. Weekly pain check-in (X1, milestone 1).**
- **Everyone, every week:** asked of every person who did or planned any workout that week, flagged or not, on both the brake and the push side. The 2× check needs outcomes for unflagged weeks too.
- **The week:** runs Monday to Sunday in the person's local time.
- **When it's asked:** a local notification on Sunday at 6 PM with the actions No / A little / Yes. The same card shows on Today until Tuesday night.
- **The one-tap answer plus OSTRC (business notes decision BN1):** the one-tap answer is recorded the moment it's tapped. "No" is recorded as an OSTRC week with no problem (all four answers zero) and needs nothing more. "A little" and "Yes" open the app to the 4 OSTRC overuse questions (Oslo Sports Trauma Research Center): taking part in running, how much training was reduced, how much performance was affected, and how bad the symptoms were. Each has its fixed answer options and a Skip. A skipped sheet keeps the one-tap answer, with no OSTRC score for that week. The OSTRC version (one body area or any health problem), its exact wording and its terms of use are confirmed with a research partner before the first cohort. The one-tap question keeps its wording unless that check changes it, because "No" has to mean "no pain or problems at all" for the zero to be valid.

**18. Sunday range report (X2, milestone 1).**
- **Computed on the phone,** from the local action log and Apple Health, so it works offline and with the harness down. For the coach page, the phone uploads its weekly counts (days counted, days in range, swaps, comebacks) as a `range_week` event. The harness shows those counts rather than recomputing them, so the coach and the runner always see the same numbers. A shared fixture tests the phone's computation.
- **When it shows:** as Today's "This week" sentence (design review decision 2), from Sunday 7 PM (or right after the pain answer) through Monday. It then returns to the weekly count. It isn't a card.
- **A day "in range":** a day counts as in range unless one of these happened:
  - **brake:** the person ran more than the swap's suggested size on a day with any flag (spike, Sore or Tired), after keeping the plan or ignoring the flag;
  - **push:** a planned workout was missed and neither it nor its comeback was done within 24 hours.
- **What else counts as in range:** rest days, days with no plan, swaps taken, and undos followed by a run no bigger than the suggested size.
- **Not counted:** a day counts only if Evergreen has some sign of it: a Health workout, a morning-check answer, or the app opened. Days with none of these are left out. This uses only data the app already has, so no step permission is needed; HealthKit returns a refused type as empty, so step data couldn't tell a refusal from an idle day. A rest day on which the app was opened counts as in range.
- **What it shows:** days in range, swaps taken and comebacks done (once milestone 2 ships). It shows the pain answer to the person only.
- **No guilt:** the report never names which days were out of range. A week where every day was left out says "Not enough data this week." A week with no out-of-range days says "In range N of N days. Nothing needed changing.", where N is the number of counted days.

**19. Apple Watch app (X3, milestone 4).**
- **Scope:** a new watchOS target with the morning check, swap accept and an "in range" complication.
- **The rule runs on the phone only.** The phone pushes today's state (the planned run, any flag and its suggestion, the week's in-range count) to the watch with WatchConnectivity's application context.
- **Recording actions:** a watch tap sends a message the phone records as the same `ActionRecord` as a Lock Screen tap (item 7). CalendarSync then writes the event. Nothing writes the calendar directly, so no change is reverted.
- **Phone out of reach:** the tap is queued with `transferUserInfo`. The watch shows "Will sync with your phone", and the complication shows the last known state.
- **Stale taps are dropped:** if a queued tap arrives after the run has started, or after the person already chose on the phone, it's dropped, and the watch shows what was decided.
- **Verified by:** a test that a queued watch accept becomes exactly one `ActionRecord` when it arrives, and a manual run on a paired simulator.

**20. Coach view (X4, milestone 3).**
- **Sharing:** the runner taps "Share with my coach". The phone calls `POST /users/{user}/coach-shares` with the secret. The harness returns a link holding a random 128-bit token and stores only the token's hash in `coach_shares`.
- **The page:** `GET /coach/{token}` is public and exempt from `require_secret`, like `/health`. It returns a server-rendered page with `noindex` and `no-store` headers.
- **What the page shows:** the last 4 weeks of swaps with their reasons (planned size, suggested size, the 30-day longest, rounded) and the weekly report.
- **What it leaves out:** calendar titles, injury details and raw Health samples.
- **How the runner felt (X7, X10):** shown only if the runner ticked "Include how I've felt" when sharing. That covers weekly pain answers (the one-tap answer only; OSTRC answers are never shown to a coach), Sore and Tired reasons, and the 30-day longest on injury-adjusted swaps. Without the tick, swaps read "Shortened: big jump" or "Shortened: how you felt", and injury-adjusted swaps show only the planned and suggested sizes. The tick is stored with the share and can be changed at any time.
- **Lifecycle:**
  - a share expires after 90 days, and the runner can renew it;
  - revoke lives in Your data under "Coach links", and a revoked or expired link shows "This link is no longer active";
  - DELETE removes every share.

**21. Return-to-running plan (X5, milestone 4).**
- **The trigger:** an onboarding injury in the last 3 months (item 4), or a "Yes" pain answer (item 17).
- **Ask first:** an entry card asks: "Want a walk-run plan back to running? 4 weeks, on the days you usually run." The card carries the not-medical-advice line and "see a physio if it hurts to walk". Nothing starts without a yes.
- **The plan:** walk-run sessions on Evergreen's calendar on days the person picks on the entry card, pre-filled from their usual run days (HistoryPlan) when they have recent runs. People in this group often have none. It starts at 20 minutes, alternating 1 minute of running with 2 of walking. Each week's longest session is no more than 10% over the previous week's, so the plan peaks at about 27 minutes, under item 5's 30-minute / 5 km return limit.
- **Exempt from the spike flags** because the plan is generated under their limits.
- **Over an external plan:** the plan wins on its days (item 1). External runs on other days are paused too, with the same note, so the person isn't running twice in the return weeks.
- **The end:** the plan ends in one of three ways:
  - when its last week is done, it hands back to the normal plan, whose first run is checked by the spike rule as usual;
  - the person taps "End return plan" (with Undo);
  - a "Yes" pain answer during the plan pauses it and suggests a physio. A paused plan's future sessions are removed, but external runs stay paused: pain is exactly when the brake matters most. Today shows "Resume your return plan" or "End it and go back to your plan". Resuming picks up at the week it paused and asks first, like starting. Ending brings the external runs back, and the first one is checked by the spike rule.

**22. Accountability buddy (X6 and X8, milestone 2).**
- **Inviting:** a push-side person creates an invite. The harness (`POST /users/{user}/buddy`) creates a `buddy_pairs` row with status invited and returns an 8-character code that expires after 48 hours and works once.
- **The friend's onboarding:** a short path: a TestFlight invite, the disclaimer, notification permission, the "What leaves your phone" consent (item 9) and the code. There's no backtest or injury question unless they later choose to use Evergreen for themselves.
- **Redeeming safely:** redeem runs `UPDATE buddy_pairs ... WHERE code=%s AND status='invited'` and checks the rowcount, because SingleStore runs in autocommit here and can't enforce every unique rule. It's refused when either person already has a paired row. A test redeems one code twice at once.
- **Joining:** the friend installs Evergreen, gets their own anonymous ID, enters the code, and both see a consent screen saying what's shared. The harness moves that `buddy_pairs` row to paired. Each person has at most one buddy.
- **What triggers a note:**
  - the observer query's completion handler is called on every code path. Background delivery is best-effort (developers report about 4 wakes an hour even at "immediate") and stops after a force-quit, which is why the app-open fallback matters;
  - the phone registers an `HKObserverQuery` for workouts, with HealthKit background delivery (`enableBackgroundDelivery`, frequency immediate), so a finished workout wakes the app;
  - the app then logs a `workout_completed` event to `guardrail_events` for every workout of the person who sent the invite (one-way, as X6 describes; keyed to the pair, so notes continue after a newcomer leaves push mode). The friend's workouts are never uploaded for this, with only its type and rounded minutes, and no comeback or plan field;
  - that event alone triggers the harness's note, so a comeback looks like any other workout.
  - If iOS delays background delivery, the note goes out the next time the app opens.
- **What's shared:** completed workouts only, as "Alex did a 15-minute walk." A comeback is shown as an ordinary completion, never called a comeback, so a miss is never revealed. Misses and booked sessions are never shared.
- **Apple push (X8):**
  - the app gains the `aps-environment` and `com.apple.developer.healthkit.background-delivery` entitlements, plus an app delegate (`UIApplicationDelegateAdaptor`, which the app doesn't have today). The delegate registers for remote notifications, receives the token, and registers the workout observer at launch;
  - the device token goes to `PUT /users/{user}/push-token` with its APNs environment (production for TestFlight builds, sandbox for Xcode builds). Both are stored in `push_tokens`, and the sender picks the matching APNs host;
  - the harness sends through APNs with a token-based key kept in a server environment variable, using the existing `httpx` (with the `h2` extra for HTTP/2) and `pyjwt`. It reuses one client and a cached JWT per warm instance, as Apple asks;
  - at most 3 notes per pair per day;
  - each note is stored as pending or sent. A note that times out is retried on the pair's next events call and dropped after 24 hours (eng review D6).
- **Removing tokens:** a token is removed on unpair, on DELETE, and when APNs answers 410 Unregistered or 400 BadDeviceToken.
- **Ending it:** either side can unpair in Your data. The other side's buddy row disappears, with no notification.
- **Waits with the push:** the buddy is gated with milestone 2 on the newcomer calls.

## Success Criteria

- **Validation:** on the backtest, the share of flagged days marked "hurt or sore" is at least twice the share for the mixed-in unflagged days. "Don't know" answers are left out of both shares. If more than 40% of answers are "Don't know", the check is inconclusive and the thresholds are set from the first weeks of live flags instead. Below 2×, change the thresholds before building more.
- **Behavior, first cohort (about 4):** every runner with an external plan sees at least one flag and accepts at least one swap in two weeks.
- **Behavior, second cohort (10):** a swap acceptance rate above 40% over two weeks. This is the north-star metric; near zero means the nudge isn't working.
- **Retention:** at least 6 of the 10 still answering the morning check in week 3.
- **Push, first newcomers (3):** over 4 weeks, each completes more workouts than their own prior 4-week normal, and at least half of comebacks are completed.
- **Evidence for YC:** each of the first runners can say, in their own words, a time Evergreen changed a run.
- **Live outcomes (item 17):** the second cohort answers at least 70% of weekly check-ins in weeks 1–4, and at least 70% of "A little" or "Yes" answers come with all four OSTRC answers. After 8 weeks, the share of "A little" or "Yes" answers in the 2 weeks after a kept flag is compared with the share after unflagged weeks. The target is 2× again. With about 10 runners this is directional, not proof.
- **Range report (item 18):** at least 6 of the 10 open it in weeks 2 and 3.
- **Watch (item 19):** among runners with a watch, at least half of morning answers come from the watch by week 2 of using it.
- **Coach view (item 20):** at least 2 runners share with a coach, and at least 1 coach asks how to add another runner.
- **Return plan (item 21):** every runner who starts one finishes it or ends it on purpose. No "Yes" pain answer comes during a plan that was followed.
- **Buddy (item 22):** at least 1 of the first 3 newcomers pairs with a friend. Paired newcomers complete a higher share of comebacks than unpaired ones (directional).

So a background EventKit write that isn't also recorded as an ActionRecord is undone on the next foreground sync. A spike suggests 'never more than the 30-day longest run' (L), or 0.9 × L after an injury, while 'Floors: never below 20 minutes for a duration plan or 3 km for a distance plan', and 'A spike always suggests the shorter run.' With L = 2 km (2+ comparable runs) and planned P = 3.5 km (> 1.10 × 2 = 2.2 km, a spike), the cap says at most 2 km and the floor says at least 3 km. With an injury and L = 3 km, the cap is 0.9 × 3 = 2.7 km against the 3 km floor. The duration case is the same: with L = 18 min, the cap is 18 and the floor is 20. The under-10% and floor-unit parts of R2-4 are fixed.

The Distribution Plan (unchanged) still says 'the planner port is milestone 2.' The two sections now disagree on whether the push or the harness port comes second, which is the sequencing the new milestone gate (newcomer calls) depends on.
4. R3-4 — feasibility (blocking): Item 8 says 'CalendarSync writes it like any plan item', but a plan item can't carry this swap today. PlanItem (Contract.swift) holds only id, slot (start, end, activityType, place), points and source (plan | comeback | routine), with no distance and no free-text title. CalendarEntry.title(for:) builds the title only from source and activityType ('Run', 'Comeback run · 2× points', 'Run (routine)'), so it can't produce 'Easy run 12 km (was 18 km)'. A distance swap (12 km in place of 18 km) has nothing to store its size in. AgentAction.Kind has no swap case either: it's bookComeback, move, shrink, addRoutine, note, purchase, cancelPurchase, purchaseOutcome, addWorkout, and action ids are normally chosen by the agent, not the phone. These are Contract.swift changes, which the README says mirror the harness's Python models ('Talk to Surya before changing a field') and the unbuilt T3 fixtures.
5. R3-5 — completeness (blocking): Item 1 says a sized all-day run's swap event 'is all-day too', but item 8 now creates every external swap as a plan item that CalendarSync writes 'like any plan item'. The plan-item path has no all-day support. Slot is start and end only, CalendarEntry has no all-day flag, and EKEvent.apply sets only url, title, notes, startDate and endDate, never isAllDay. EventKit also normalizes an all-day event's start and end to midnight boundaries, so if one were written, CalendarChanges would see it differ from its entry and rewrite it on every sync. As specified, the all-day swap is either written as a timed event or churns.
6. R3-6 — feasibility (blocking): The swap's identity, and the 'source event later moves or is deleted' check, rest on 'the source event id'. The codebase deliberately never keys on EKEvent.eventIdentifier (CalendarEntry.swift: '`eventIdentifier` is never used as a key'), because it isn't guaranteed stable across calendar syncs. Runna and coach plans usually arrive as read-only subscriptions (Constraints), which refresh by re-publishing their events. If the identifier changes on a refresh, the source looks deleted, the swap is undone, and a new id appears with no swap. The design doesn't say which identifier is meant or how it behaves across a subscription refresh.
7. R3-7 — consistency (blocking): The change from 'the swap is dropped' to 'the swap action is undone' makes a system-driven reversal look like the person's Undo. A long run moved to another day, still a spike, then goes unprotected, while the person never undid anything.

#### Section 4: Data flow and interaction edge cases

```
 Health workout ─► observer wake ─► is paired? ─no─► stop
                                       │yes
                                       ▼
                        workout_completed event ─► queue ─► harness ─► APNs ─► friend

                  duplicate wake → event id = HK workout UUID, deduped on harness

```

**Async ordering, for the invariant "one recorded decision per run per flag":**

- **Phone tap vs watch tap:** the phone and watch can both answer the same flag. Order A: the phone's Keep lands first, then the watch's Accept arrives, is seen as stale and dropped. Order B: the watch's Accept lands first, then the phone's Keep shows as an Undo of the Accept.
- **The mechanism:** the action log is keyed by (run key, flag kind), and it accepts the first answer. A later answer from another device is an explicit Undo/redo on the phone, never a silent overwrite.
- **Owner must prove** both orders with controlled pause and release in a test.

| Interaction | Edge case | Handled? |
|---|---|---|
| Swap from the Lock Screen, phone and watch | Double tap | Yes: the first answer wins (above) |
| Pain check-in | Answered after the week rolled over | Yes: it's stored against the week it was asked for, not the current one |
| Coach share | The runner toggles pain answers while the coach has the page open | Yes: the next load reflects it, with `no-store` |
| Buddy invite | Both people send each other codes | Yes: the first redeemed code pairs them, and the other becomes invalid (one buddy each) |
| Return plan | An injury reported again during the plan | Yes: a "Yes" pauses the plan (item 21) |
| Range report | A week with no counted days | Yes: the report says "Not enough data this week" instead of "0 of 0". **Added to item 18's copy rules by this review**, as a direct consequence of the approved definition. |
